# Naming and App Store positioning

Reviewed 2 November 2026.

## Selected name

- **Primary English app and extension name:** Short Video Blocker (19 characters).
- **English subtitle:** Hide images. Keep your focus. (29 characters).
- **Positioning:** selective filtering of distracting videos and images, while preserving useful posts and regular YouTube videos. Emphasize choice and attention rather than guilt or claims to cure addiction.
- **Selected icon:** a deep-purple prohibition circle and Play symbol on a pale lavender macOS tile. It uses no platform logos or text. The earlier abstract veil concept was replaced to make video blocking immediately recognizable.

The user explicitly prefers a functional search-query name over a coined brand. The initial **Feedveil** proposal was therefore discarded as the primary name. Its preliminary absence from visible search results was a name-conflict observation, not evidence of search demand.

Translate supporting copy and functional naming appropriately for each locale. App Store titles and subtitles must independently fit within 30 characters. Preserve existing technical bundle identifiers and storage keys so the display-name change does not intentionally reset the installed extension or preferences.

## Metadata strategy and limitations

Apple states that search considers title, subtitle, keyword and category relevance alongside user behavior. Its [App Store search guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/) recommends accurate audience terms, no duplicate words across name/subtitle/keyword fields, and a 100-character keyword field. [Product-page guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/) recommends memorable, distinctive names and informative supporting copy rather than keyword stuffing.

The selected generic title prioritizes immediate functional clarity over brand distinctiveness. Apple recommends avoiding overly generic or confusingly similar names; availability and review acceptance still need confirmation in App Store Connect. This decision is not a reserved name, trademark clearance, legal opinion, or an App Review guarantee. No name was reserved and no trademark application was filed.

The subtitle **Hide images. Keep your focus.** adds the separate image-filtering feature and a benefit without repeating “short”, “video” or “blocker”. An illustrative keyword-field candidate is `scroll,distraction,filter,media,attention,clutter,browse,control,calm` (69 characters). It is a relevance hypothesis, not a demand-ranked recommendation. Validate separately by storefront and language rather than applying US Mac scores to all 15 locales.

## Third-party names

Apple restricts using another developer's brand or product name in an app's name or icon without approval and prohibits unauthorized trademark stuffing. Names and subtitles are limited to 30 characters. See [App Review Guidelines 2.3.7, 4.1(c), and 5.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) and [App Store Connect app information](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-information/).

Keep Facebook, YouTube and Reels out of this app's own name, subtitle and icon. Meta's [trademark overview](https://www.meta.com/brand/resources/meta/our-trademarks/) lists Facebook and Reels and restricts incorporating Meta marks into another brand. “Shorts” can also identify YouTube's feature in this product category; the selected title uses the generic phrase “short video” rather than depending on that association. This is a risk-reduction choice, not a conclusion that every use of the ordinary word “shorts” is prohibited.

Necessary factual references to supported websites belong in functionality descriptions and controls, without suggesting affiliation or endorsement. YouTube's [API branding guidelines](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/branding-guidelines) distinguish descriptive compatibility language from using YouTube or variants in an application's overall name. Those guidelines apply to API clients; this DOM-filtering extension does not obtain a blanket trademark license from them. Apple's separate third-party-service requirements remain applicable.

## Suggested English description

Short Video Blocker makes your feed easier to choose. Hide videos and images in Facebook feeds, and hide YouTube Shorts while keeping regular YouTube videos available.

Choose each filter independently. Changes apply to open tabs, and your preferences stay on your device. Works in Safari on macOS; it does not change the Facebook or YouTube apps.

Short Video Blocker is an independent product and is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by Google or Meta. Third-party product names belong to their respective owners.

Describe only shipped behavior. Do not claim complete removal of every future format, prevention of every media download, or operation inside native social-media apps.
